The Ukrainian underground

Six waves, from the Kyiv avant-garde to the wartime scene. Tap a name to see it on the map, where these notes are marked with a yellow UA.

What the Ukrainian line adds
  1. The system here is often political, not aesthetic. The Western avant-garde chose serialism for itself, while in Soviet Ukraine the "correct" form was imposed by the authorities and modernism was condemned as "formalism". Turning to timbre, silence and homemade sound was therefore also a form of resistance.
  2. The underground hid in applied and domestic forms. Cartoons, theatre, cassettes, apartment recordings. There was nowhere even to perform an ideal work, so process and sensation became the only available path.
  3. The archive builds the canon, often from outside. Cukor Bila Smert, Nianio, Yurchenko and Goncharova became known decades later, through reissues for Western markets. It is the same mechanism as Mendelssohn reviving Bach in 1829, and it contradicts the idea that "sensation" has freed itself from the canon.
  4. In wartime, attention becomes a matter of survival. Listening to the city, the alerts and the silence is no longer an aesthetic practice but a daily necessity. Music becomes testimony and memory, as in Kyiv Eternal.

An honest note: most available sources are in English and focus on Kyiv and Kharkiv. The Lviv, Odesa and Dnipro scenes of the 1990s and 2000s are less documented, so this part of the map is surely incomplete. If you know who is missing, it is worth adding.

    How to read the map

    Three lanes, like three musical staves: system (ideal and rules), process (system as a tool) and sensation (sound, body, listening). Each note is a work, a studio or an event. Lines show influence or reaction.

    "Listen" opens a YouTube search, "Read" opens Wikipedia or the project's site, and books open in Open Library. The yellow UA mark stands for the Ukrainian scene.
